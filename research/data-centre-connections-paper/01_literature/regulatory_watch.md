## Bulletin log (newest first; one dated paragraph per week, nil returns recorded)

**2026-08-24 (covers 20 to 24 August). TRIPWIRE: CALIBRATION ANCHOR MOVED.** (1) Coverage of AEMO's 2026 Electricity Statement of Opportunities reports prospective data centre projects had generated **66.9 GW of grid connection requests as of June 2026**, up from the 44 GW reported in the 2025 IASR that anchors the evidence store's calibration funnel (PAR-0001). The funnel's top number has moved by half again in about a year; the primary (AEMO 2026 ESOO) must be obtained and extracted before Gate H2 so calibration targets are set against the current stock, and the growth rate of the request stock itself is now a modellable object. (2) Australia, mechanism detail: reporting on the AEMC advice adds that data centres would become registered market participants with connection agreements encouraging demand shifting and co-location, which moves the obligation package into connection-agreement design, the paper's core territory; separately AEMO lodged a rule change request on 21 July 2026 for operational integration and visibility of large inverter-based loads (the fourth plank of the Korte agenda becoming a rule change). (3) Texas: ERCOT told the PUCT open meeting of 14 August that the Abbott-directed data centre verification will take several months but under nine, aims to complete the audit by December, and has stopped approving new data centre connections in the interim; Batch Zero classifications remain unissued and the Texas benchmark is in limbo. Implementation risk of screening mechanisms is now a documented, quantifiable feature of the Texas case. (4) FERC: filings summaries confirm the proposed federal definition of a large load (at least 50 MW peak at a single site, connecting at 69 kV or above, not co-located) and that intervenor comments on the six RTO filings are due about mid-September. No new GB or Ireland developments detected.

**2026-08-20 (covers 29 July to 20 August). TRIPWIRE FOLLOW-THROUGH AND ONE NEW TRIPWIRE.** (1) The AEMC advice behind the 29 July ECMC agreement was publicly released on 5 August under the title "Data centres to bring clean, firm energy, be flexible and pay their way": data centres would be mandated to offset consumption by surrendering REGO certificates linked to new renewable generation, and new data centres must demonstrate to the AER that sufficient firm contracts cover their load. Timeline hardened: NER rule change requests go to ECMC in September 2026 and federal legislation is targeted for early 2027. Primary sources (AEMC media release and the Detailed Policy Assessment PDF) are now public and law firm analyses flag accompanying grid connection and cost recovery rules; extraction is a Phase 1 priority. (2) New tripwire, Texas calibration anchor: on 3 August Governor Abbott directed ERCOT not to issue Batch Zero classification notices on the original 7 August date pending a verification process for data centre loads; ERCOT is evaluating 326 projects totalling about 205 GW (twice its own roughly 100 GW projection), with a 31 August deadline to cure deficient dynamic data. Political intervention in a maturity-gated batch process is itself a mechanism-design datum for the paper (implementation risk of screening mechanisms). (3) FERC: the six RTO/ISO show cause responses were due and arrived on 17 August; six regional large-load tariff proposals are now on the docket and become new primary sources for the mechanism benchmark table; substance to be extracted next cycle. (4) No new GB or Ireland developments detected. Note on cadence: this is the first automated bulletin since 29 July; the log has a three-week span rather than weekly entries, so treat this paragraph as covering the full window.

**2026-07-29. TWO TRIPWIRES FLAGGED (condition 2, Australian mechanism status).** (1) On 28 July 2026 the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council welcomed AEMC advice on data centre regulatory pathways (requested by ECMC 8 May 2026; AEMC published a Detailed Policy Assessment, July 2026) and agreed to progress regulatory arrangements mandating that data centres offset their electricity demand through new renewable generation via a guarantee of origin scheme. The AEMC advice recommends four staged, size-differentiated obligations: surrender of REGO certificates from new additional generators; demonstration that demand is backed by new firm capacity; demand flexibility; and efficient location and connection. This is Irish-style additionality arriving in Australia and materially updates the institutional framing: the AEMC agenda has hardened from a declared direction into ministerially endorsed regulatory development. The institutional map, mechanism benchmark table and the paper's "no implemented allocation mechanism" framing must incorporate it. (2) NSW has given the state energy minister REZ-style powers to control grid access for large load infrastructure; Transgrid separately reports more than 8 GW of prospective data centre load in advanced connection discussions. A state-level allocation instrument now exists in the paper's largest pipeline state. Calendar items: FERC RTO resource adequacy reports were filed about 20 July (tariff responses due about 17 August); ERCOT's Batch Zero process took effect 11 July 2026, with about 205 GW of large load eligible as of 28 July (against roughly 438 GW tracked, an early read on the screening effect of maturity criteria) and classification results due to TSPs/DSPs by 7 August. No GB or Ireland developments detected in this sweep. New sources registered: AEMC advice PDF, ECMC outcome coverage, NSW powers coverage, ERCOT market notices.
