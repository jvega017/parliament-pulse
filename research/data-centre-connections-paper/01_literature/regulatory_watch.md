## Bulletin log (newest first; one dated paragraph per week, nil returns recorded)

**2026-07-29. TWO TRIPWIRES FLAGGED (condition 2, Australian mechanism status).** (1) On 28 July 2026 the Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council welcomed AEMC advice on data centre regulatory pathways (requested by ECMC 8 May 2026; AEMC published a Detailed Policy Assessment, July 2026) and agreed to progress regulatory arrangements mandating that data centres offset their electricity demand through new renewable generation via a guarantee of origin scheme. The AEMC advice recommends four staged, size-differentiated obligations: surrender of REGO certificates from new additional generators; demonstration that demand is backed by new firm capacity; demand flexibility; and efficient location and connection. This is Irish-style additionality arriving in Australia and materially updates the institutional framing: the AEMC agenda has hardened from a declared direction into ministerially endorsed regulatory development. The institutional map, mechanism benchmark table and the paper's "no implemented allocation mechanism" framing must incorporate it. (2) NSW has given the state energy minister REZ-style powers to control grid access for large load infrastructure; Transgrid separately reports more than 8 GW of prospective data centre load in advanced connection discussions. A state-level allocation instrument now exists in the paper's largest pipeline state. Calendar items: FERC RTO resource adequacy reports were filed about 20 July (tariff responses due about 17 August); ERCOT's Batch Zero process took effect 11 July 2026, with about 205 GW of large load eligible as of 28 July (against roughly 438 GW tracked, an early read on the screening effect of maturity criteria) and classification results due to TSPs/DSPs by 7 August. No GB or Ireland developments detected in this sweep. New sources registered: AEMC advice PDF, ECMC outcome coverage, NSW powers coverage, ERCOT market notices.
