# Novelty map

**Status:** seeded 16 July 2026 from the verified research run; Phase 1 confirms, extends or overturns.

## The claimed square

A mechanism-design treatment of load-side connection capacity as a priced option, integrating uncertain staged demand, private maturity information and heterogeneous flexibility, applied to Australian institutions where no allocation reform yet exists.

## Five closest works and the marginal contribution against each

| # | Work | What it does | What it does not do (our margin) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Johnston, Liu & Yang (2023), NBER WP 31946 | Dynamic structural model of the US generation interconnection queue; screening and flat entry fees raise completed capacity | Generation-side; queue-institution-specific; no private-information mechanism comparison; no load-side or Australian application |
| 2 | Norris, Profeta, Patino-Echeverri & Cowie-Haskell (2025), Duke Nicholas Institute | Empirical headroom: 76 to 126 GW of US load integrable at 0.25 to 1.0 per cent curtailment | Engineering headroom, not allocation or pricing; no mechanism |
| 3 | Knittel, Senga & Wang (2025/2026), MIT CEEPR WP 2025-14 / NBER w34065 / iScience | Capacity-expansion value of temporal DC flexibility; emissions can rise in low-renewables grids | No connection-capacity scarcity; no allocation problem |

## Pre-committed kill criterion (Gate H1)

If Phase 1 finds a paper that prices load-side connection capacity as an option under private maturity information, narrow to the Australian institutional application or drop to the fallback ladder. Comparative description of overseas reforms alone does not clear the bar.

## Watch items

- Working papers spawned by the FERC show cause proceedings (economists will be drafting now).
- LBNL queue statistics updates; any load-side queue dataset release.
