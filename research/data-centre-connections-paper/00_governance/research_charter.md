**Status:** DRAFT, awaiting Gate H0 sign-off by the Human Research Director.
**Version:** 1.0, 16 July 2026.

## Question

How should Australian electricity networks reserve, expand and price scarce locational grid capacity when prospective data-centre loads are large, staged, uncertain and partly flexible?

## Intended contribution

A mechanism-design treatment of load-side connection capacity as a priced option, integrating uncertain staged demand, private maturity information and heterogeneous flexibility, applied to Australian institutions where no allocation reform yet exists. Must beat the five closest works named in `01_literature/novelty_map.md`.

## Primary output

A model-led Australian institutional working paper with calibrated simulations and a descriptive empirical module built from public data. Causal empirics are an upgrade path, not the promise. Fallback ladder per plan section 9.4.

## Audience and venue

Australian economics or energy-policy working paper first. Journal class, reference style and length locked at Gate H1.

## Scope boundaries

- Data centres under AI-driven demand uncertainty, not "LLM data centres".
- Both connection levels (distribution and transmission) modelled or an explicit scoping decision recorded at H2.
- No confidential government, network or commercial information. Public sources only unless the Director documents authority and a disclosure plan.
- Electrolysers and other large loads as robustness cases only.

## Conflict and disclosure protocol

- The author's professional proximity to Queensland Government energy matters is disclosed in the working paper and every public derivative: views are personal and do not represent the Queensland Government or any employer.
- No use of information obtained through employment that is not public.
- Interim public commentary (including any LinkedIn series) is framed as questions and design problems, carries the personal-capacity disclaimer, and states no quantitative result before Gate H4.
- Conflicted sources (for example the AWS-commissioned phantom-demand briefing) are used only with the conflict attributed in text.
