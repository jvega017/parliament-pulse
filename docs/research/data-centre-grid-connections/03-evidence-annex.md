# Evidence annex

Supporting evidence for `01-critical-review.md` and `02-improved-plan-v2.md`.
Research run: 16 July 2026. Method: five parallel search angles (Australian regulatory state of play; international queue reforms; academic literature; sceptical/phantom-demand evidence; Australian calibration data and seed-source verification), 22 sources fetched, 95 claims extracted, top 25 submitted to three-vote adversarial verification.

**Limitation, disclosed:** a session rate limit interrupted the verification stage. 12 claims were adversarially confirmed (3-0 or 2-0 votes, none refuted); 13 further claims lost their verification votes to the rate limit and are reported below as **single-extraction**: quoted directly from a fetched source by one extraction agent, but not independently re-verified. No claim was refuted. Three fetches failed or returned nothing usable (NESO connections-reform page, an assumed AEMO 2026 ISP PDF URL, and an AEMO newsroom page); the affected seed sources are marked TO BE CONFIRMED rather than assumed.

## B. Single-extraction claims (quoted from fetched primary or reputable secondary sources; verification votes lost to rate limit; none refuted)

**Australia**

- Step Change calibration: data centre consumption growing at 25.1 per cent average annual rate to 12.0 TWh by FY30 and 34.5 TWh by FY50; FY25 baseline 3.9 TWh (1.4 TWh in FY18), about 2 per cent of NEM grid-supplied consumption; over 98 per cent distribution-connected, about 1.5 per cent transmission-connected. (AEMO-hosted Oxford Economics report.)
- AEMC extended the Package 2 final determination to 29 October 2026 due to submission complexity. (aemc.gov.au.)
- Draft standards deliberately harmonised with Texas, Ireland and Finland; motivated partly by a US incident where about 60 data centres consuming about 1,500 MW disconnected simultaneously. (aemc.gov.au.)
- "Data | Power: Navigating policy, regulation and networks" is a speech by AEMC Commissioner Rainer Korte, delivered 28 April 2026 in Melbourne, published on the AEMC website. Central message: existing access standards are ill-suited to large inverter-based loads; large data-centre loads behave under disturbance more like generators than traditional loads. No phantom-demand quantification appears in the speech itself. (aemc.gov.au.)
- AEMO Quarterly Energy Dynamics, March quarter 2026: first published NEM data-centre pipeline statistics; 11 projects above 5 MW totalling 5.4 GW of maximum demand in the transmission connection process; about 60 per cent NSW and 40 per cent Victoria; about two years application to energisation; AEMO assumes 5 to 10 year ramp-up. (Secondary source citing the QED; confirm against the AEMO primary.)
- DISR "Expectations of data centres and AI infrastructure developers" released 23 March 2026; five expectations including paying full grid-connection costs, underwriting additional clean generation and demand flexibility; enforced via prioritisation in Commonwealth regulatory assessments. (industry.gov.au.)
- United Energy December 2025 business case (AER-published): 27 proponent requests totalling about 1,679 MW across the Victorian Power Networks group since November 2024; expected capacity 44 per cent below total pipeline; FY27 committed capacity about 1 GW, about 60 per cent of AEMO's FY31 forecast; AER draft determination accepted an 85 per cent customer contribution rate; from 1 July 2026 high-voltage and sub-transmission connections liable for tax on capital contributions; pipeline assessment by Mandala using known applications as of October 2025. (aer.gov.au PDF; quotes from search-indexed text, direct fetch blocked by the proxy; confirm by direct access.)

**United States**

- FERC, 18 June 2026: six show cause orders under FPA section 206 to PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO; 60 days to justify tariffs or file changes (about 17 August 2026); five reform categories (efficient study processes, cost-shift prevention and transparency, co-location and behind-the-meter generation, new transmission services for flexible large loads, study process for generators serving proximate or co-located loads); resource adequacy reports due about 20 July 2026; Chairman Laura Swett; deliberately chosen over a NOPR for speed. (ferc.gov.)
- Texas: PUCT voted 12 March 2026 to publish draft rule 16 TAC §25.194 implementing SB6 for loads of 75 MW or more; comments due 17 April 2026. Draft includes a non-refundable interconnection fee of USD 50,000 per MW of contracted peak demand, financial security, study fees, 100 per cent up-front payment of direct interconnection costs; mandatory disclosure of parallel or substantially similar requests by the same company or affiliates with defined materiality thresholds (one-year delay, 20 per cent demand change, changed point of interconnection); site control extending at least five years beyond the contracted peak-demand date; SB6 mandates remote-curtailment capability for loads interconnected after 31 December 2025 and a competitively procured demand-reduction reliability service. (Greenberg Traurig client alert.)
- ERCOT: PUCT approved "Batch Zero" protocol revisions 18 June 2026; single clustered study of qualified 75 MW plus projects replacing project-by-project evaluation; eligibility gated by maturity criteria satisfiable by affiliate attestation; curtailable-connection pathway for earlier access; batch inclusion notices August 2026; transmission plan expected fall 2027; ERCOT tracking more than 438 GW of large-load requests, nearly 90 per cent from data centres. (Utility Dive.)

**Great Britain and Ireland**

- GB TMO4+ "First Ready, First Connected": replaces first-come-first-served; Gate 2 requires exclusive land rights and, for DCO-route projects, a validated planning application; demand projects (including data centres) are deemed "needed" and face only the readiness test; transitional protections for 2026-commissioning and significantly progressed projects; Ofgem approved the proposals in 2025. (Norton Rose Fulbright briefing.)
- Ireland: CRU decision CRU/2025/236 published 12 December 2025, superseding CRU/21/124; new data centres must provide dispatchable generation and/or storage, onsite or electrically proximate, matched to maximum import capacity and participating in the wholesale market; at least 80 per cent of annual demand from additional Republic-of-Ireland renewables with a six-year glide path (threshold 1 MVA maximum import capacity); location-specific constrained/unconstrained assessment replaces regional screening; system operators must regularly publish available-capacity information. (cru.ie.)

**Academic literature**

- Johnston, Liu and Yang, NBER WP 31946 (December 2023): most wind and solar entrants to the US interconnection queue never complete; longer queues causally increase waiting times; high interconnection costs drive withdrawal; dynamic structural model; counterfactuals show screening out some applicants and flat entry fees both increase completed capacity. Generation-side, not load-side. (nber.org.)
- Norris, Profeta, Patino-Echeverri and Cowie-Haskell, Duke Nicholas Institute, published 13 February 2025: 76 GW of new US load integrable at 0.25 per cent average annual curtailment, 98 GW at 0.5 per cent, 126 GW at 1.0 per cent; average curtailment durations 1.7 to 2.5 hours; nearly 90 per cent of curtailment hours retain at least half the load; ERCOT alone about 15 GW; methodology covers 22 balancing authorities serving about 95 per cent of US peak load; Norris testified to a US House subcommittee March 2025. (nicholasinstitute.duke.edu.)
- Knittel, Senga and Wang: MIT CEEPR WP 2025-14 (July 2025), also NBER w34065, published in iScience (2026) as "Flexible data centers reduce power system costs but can increase emissions"; GenX capacity-expansion model, three US regions; cost savings up to about 5 per cent (Texas), 4 per cent (Mid-Atlantic), 2 per cent (WECC); emissions can rise about 3 per cent in low-renewables grids. Temporal shifting, not capacity allocation. (ceepr.mit.edu.)
- Kay, Reaser and Taylor, "Processing Power: The Effect of Data Centers on Wholesale Electricity Markets", Dallas Fed WP 2606, March 2026 (also SSRN): existing data centres raised US wholesale prices about 3 to 5 per cent on average, more in data-centre corridors; through 2028, about 50 per cent price rise under high-utilisation build-out versus about 20 per cent moderate; hourly unit-level least-cost dispatch model. Companion Dallas Fed Economics article (Kay, Kilian and Taylor, 5 March 2026): data centres add 0.05 percentage points to headline PCE inflation in 2026 and 0.13 points in 2030 under peak-concentrated utilisation. (dallasfed.org.)
- Riepin, Zavala and Brown, "Spatio-temporal load shifting for truly clean computing", Advances in Applied Energy 17 (2025), 100202 (arXiv 2405.00036): 24/7 carbon-free matching cost falls 1.29 plus or minus 0.07 EUR/MWh per additional percentage point of flexible load; three physical value signals identified; model and data open source (GitHub Irieo/space-time-optimization; Zenodo 10869650). (arxiv.org.)
